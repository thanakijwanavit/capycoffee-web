The ladder

Three depths for Freebird

We do not run a roast rainbow. Light, medium, dark — named for the capy at that age. Same lot. Same Nano 7. Different time in the heat.

1.0 · light

Young Capy 1.0

Dropped early, while Freebird still smells like the wet mill. Peach skin, jasmine, a little cane sugar. Best as pour-over or a clean AeroPress. If you like milk, Adult Capy will treat you better.

Cue: pale caramel, high aroma, short development.

Drink: V60, batch brew, tasting cup.

Cue: even crack, honey color, balanced sweetness.

Drink: filter, AeroPress, mellow espresso.

3.0 · medium

Adult Capy 3.0

The house paddle. Freebird’s stone fruit folds into toasted almond and cocoa nib. Clear enough to taste the lot, round enough for breakfast. If you are unsure, start here.

5.0 · dark

Grandpa Capy 5.0

A longer soak. Molasses, cedar, bitter chocolate — still Freebird, just wearing a heavier coat. Built for espresso and evening milk drinks. We stop before ash; Grandpa is dark, not burnt.

Cue: full crack, oils just thinking about it.

Drink: espresso, milk, French press.
